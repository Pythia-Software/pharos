Retired definitions remain resolvable for saved collections. All 96 original concepts remain below, with their richer requested behavior and blockers preserved. “Partial implementation” means the current card answers a narrower observed question; it does not claim to deliver the complete original concept.

Recommendations require matching results in explicit 1, 7, 30 or 90 day lookbacks. New queries store native datetime operands such as `>= -7d` and `< +0s` in URLs, snapshots and saved views. These are elapsed windows through execution time, rather than midnight-aligned calendar spans. A day means 24 hours across daylight-saving changes. Usage is filtered at its recorded fact grain and is not prorated into imaginary partial rows. Old bookmarks explicitly marked as rolling are migrated to native operands; intentionally fixed saved ranges remain intact. Titles, descriptions, ordering and collected cover settings use `pharos-bookmarks-v1`; old saved-query IDs are preserved.

The machine-readable companion [homepage-query-cards.json](homepage-query-cards.json) retains every original recipe, eligibility rule, proposed preview, implementation mapping and capability request. Update both files when a deferred card is implemented.

## Design conventions for subsequent card studies

Carry these preferences into each new category:

- Start with a human goal: what the viewer wants to understand about their effort, attention, or contribution. Give each group a clear thesis and remove redundant or weakly interpretable cards.
- Prefer descriptive metric titles under five words, except explicitly requested names. Use roughly fifteen words of description to identify the actual period, population, averaging, and important caveats. The CTA starts with an encouraging verb and names what opens. Avoid question-and-answer titles, vague “this window” wording, and descriptions of rendering mechanics such as “with a sparkline.”
- Put a headline’s unit beside its value when it fits, with shrinking or wrapping for long values. Make sparklines fill the available plot space. Place numeric high, low, and norm ticks on the left without redundant labels; keep the norm dashed, the range shading subtle and free of boundary rules, and markers circular at every width.
- Choose the chart for the interpretation: pies describe additive parts of a complete total, with an exact Other remainder; bars compare rates and averages; distributions need useful bins that remain readable despite outliers; scatterplots should express an understandable relationship and expose their sample sizes.
- Hover details show the actual date, precise value, bin count, or share. Keep them outside clipped covers, preserve whole-card navigation, and omit distracting population totals from histogram tooltips. Use the calculation’s fixed timestamp for cached trend dates.
- Cards stay still on hover and focus. Use the same compact legend structure for bars and pies, uniform circle scaling, standard model colors, and a separate sequential purple palette for effort.
- Box plots share one axis across cohorts and grow with visible rows instead of using a fixed tall cover. Label medians compactly; retain means, quartiles, ranges, and sample sizes on hover. Apply explicit sample cutoffs after any intended population filter, and keep rare extremes discoverable in a separate ranked view.
- Use log–log scatterplots when workloads span orders of magnitude. Plot raw coordinates, disclose omitted nonpositive points, and keep those observations inspectable in the table. Distinguish recorded tokens from text-size estimates and filter each event at its own timestamp before aggregation.
- The collection FAB stays square with softly rounded corners and a translucent fill. Position it absolutely in the card’s bottom-right corner; allow overlap and reserve no caption height for it.
- Define denominators and identities before interpreting a metric. Keep human writing distinct from supplied context and automation, use ratios of window totals for averages, preserve missing-data gaps, and avoid inferring quality, productivity, or resulting code changes from writing volume or literal cues.
- Feedback typing must not rerun queries or chart formatting. Reuse the shared review component, memoize previews independently of feedback, debounce draft writes, and flush the latest draft before exporting or leaving.
- Review a category together on a live page, with feedback for individual cards, groups, and the whole set plus a copyable report. Preserve stable IDs and earlier feedback drafts; retire rejected definitions from discovery while keeping saved cards resolvable.

Scatterplot candidates discussed, not yet implemented: human messages per conversation versus words per message; conversation count versus words per conversation; words per message versus concrete-feedback share; and authored-word share versus supplied-context message share. Each dot would represent a repository over the selected period, with the underlying sample counts on hover.

## Human word card study — review 3 (October 15, 2026)

Home and the card library link to `/home?cards=human-review`. The third review presents 12 active human-topic cards under four groups: writing volume in context; explanation depth and its trend; distribution, authorship, and timing; and evidence of substantive contribution. Titles are descriptive, with the selected lookback interpolated into the daily writing title. `work-writing`, `human-conversation-messages`, `conversation-writing`, `human-action-phrases`, and `human-contribution-patterns` are retired from the review, library and suggestion pool after user feedback. Existing saved collections can still resolve those definitions.

**Card copy.** Each active card uses a concise metric name, a roughly fifteen-word description naming the selected period and human-writing population, and a verb-led CTA describing its destination. The user’s two explicitly requested longer names are retained. Names and descriptions interpolate the chosen window; changing from seven to thirty days does not leave a weekly label or vague “this window” description. The weekly-volume destination includes a native daily human-word metric, matching “Explore daily writing trends.”

**Historical context.** Word totals and per-message/per-conversation averages display a sparkline of 31 daily endpoints. Every point uses the same trailing N-day window as the current card. The 30-day norm is the mean of the prior 30 endpoint values. Zero-writing volume windows contribute zero; windows with an empty average denominator remain gaps. Ratio values divide the endpoint’s totals, rather than averaging constituent message averages. Trend covers place numeric ticks for the observed maximum, minimum, and thirty-day norm on the left, aligned with the shaded range and dashed reference, without redundant text labels. Equal values share one tick, and nearby labels separate without moving the plotted values. The plot fills the remaining height of a taller cover, with minimal internal spacing and range padding. The shaded range has no top/bottom outline; the dashed norm remains. SVG coordinates follow the measured plot dimensions so endpoint and hover markers stay circular at every card width. Percentage trends retain percentage formatting. Hovering shows the endpoint date, value, and trailing-window end time from the calculation’s fixed timestamp, including when cached. Gaps report an empty population rather than an interpolated value. The data request includes N + 30 days and uses one fixed execution timestamp for every page and card in the batch.

**Workspace correction.** Authorship `work_id` identifies a folded work record, which can still be one conversation within a worktree. The workspace average joins these IDs to Library locations and merges exact repository + directory identities. Different work IDs and conversations in one directory therefore contribute to one denominator. Unmapped directories are excluded from both numerator and denominator and their message count is disclosed. The metric is labelled “Words per workspace directory” because directories need not be one task or one branch.

**Charts.** `human-message-lengths` retains its historical ID but now displays a horizontal conversation-size profile. Fixed bands are 1–49, 50–199, 200–499, 500–999, 1,000–1,999, and 2,000+ human words per conversation during the selected period. Each bar shows the percentage and count of conversations in that band; extreme lengths cannot stretch a numeric axis and flatten the other bars. The grouped destination preserves exact conversation totals. Alternate covers or edited group calculations retain their native preview. Repository word totals use a pie with seven named repositories and an Other slice for the complete remainder; slices use the full human-word total as their denominator. The legend shows counts and shares, and slices and legend entries support hover details. Per-repository averages and authorship rates remain bars, because their values are not additive parts of one whole; authored-word share shows eight repositories. Writing by local hour combines the whole window into 24 hour-of-day buckets in the device timezone, including zero hours and repeated daylight-saving hours.

**Card layout.** Headline values and their units share a baseline and wrap when needed. Collection buttons are square with softly rounded corners and a translucent fill, positioned absolutely as floating actions in the card’s bottom-right corner. Captions use their content height instead of reserving space for the button; overlap is allowed.

**Chart inspection.** All card covers share unclipped pointer tooltips: daily columns show the date and each series value, hour bars show the local hour and human-word total, conversation bands show counts and shares, and native charts expose their mark details (including histogram bin bounds and counts). Distribution hover details omit the overall sample total; conversation bands retain the bin count and percentage, and hour bars retain their word total and timezone. Ranked native lists show full group names and unabridged numeric values. Tooltips clear on pointer exit, scroll, resize, Escape, or click, and preserve whole-card navigation. Native chart marks do not add tab stops inside the card link.

**Contribution evidence.** `human-contribution-patterns` is retired after the second review because its broad catch-all category was not informative. `human-specific-feedback` requires a direction/correction/constraint cue in typed spans plus a concrete detail cue: a reason, condition, number, quoted target, path, link, paste or attachment. Short messages can qualify. Injected instructions and copied agent text cannot supply the authored cue. Concrete feedback now includes the same 31-endpoint trailing-period percentage trend and thirty-endpoint norm as the writing averages, recomputing its assessed-message denominator at every endpoint. These are inspectable English-language rules, not semantic quality classifications; representative message links are shown with the review cards.

`human-example-phrases` also retains its historical ID but replaces literal “for example” detection with observed images/screenshots, likely pasted text/code, and file references/links. A message can contribute to several format rows. Its denominator is assessed human contributions, including context-only messages, rather than inferred code/file changes. The review also shows percentages and numerator/denominator counts within the five most active mapped workspace directories. Clipped messages are excluded from cue/format assessment and their count is disclosed. No outcome, acceptance or code-change attribution is inferred from a supplied reference.

The derived covers read the existing authenticated rows API; no new persistent annotations or backend schema are needed. Message evidence is shared within an execution, paged at 500 rows, and limited to 20,000 matching source messages. Oversized, incomplete, changing-total or failed loads produce an explicit error instead of a partial score. Directory lookups use exact observed work IDs. Normal source filters are retained for the derived calculation. Grouped, searched, fixed-range or alternate-cover bookmarks continue to use their native query preview.

Keep / Change / Drop and free-text feedback are available per card, per group and for the whole set. Review 3 drafts use browser key `pharos-human-word-review-v3`, preserving the previous two stored drafts. Copy feedback exports the current review version, titles, stable IDs, verdicts, notes and selected window. View report provides a clipboard fallback. Feedback itself does not edit a card definition.

## Integration stages

1. Integrated: local Miami V3 SQLite/map execution, canonical computed metadata/revisions, admitted computed source filters, row-producing groups/HAVING, result-stage distributions, timezone-aware calendar metrics and compact native covers.
2. Preview scheduling: compatible metrics share one request and snapshot, with unique wire IDs and a maximum of 20 metrics per batch. Three workers bound scans; cached results retain all metric payloads for one minute. Metadata refreshes on each uncached run. Leaving Home cancels work. Local diagnostics and definitively rejected batches isolate failing cards.
3. Remaining: derived message, episode, interval, task/cohort and lineage datasets; bounded evidence hydration; arbitrary percentiles, heatmaps and typed adapter seams where the full ideas need them.

Each HTTP preview request has a 15-second deadline. Saved queries keep their scope, including shown-row pages; suggested metrics default to all matching rows. A bookmark's optional cover metric ID changes only its preview. Compact native covers show at most four result groups (24 for scatter/line/vertical bars), disclose that budget, and retain complete returned results for selection; the full view keeps every saved metric. Existing daily Usage columns retain local-calendar gaps, the shared palette and an exact Other series.

Recommendation-specific eligibility is evaluated separately from saved-view readiness. Empty saved views remain in the collection; zero rates with observations are valid saved results. Rates include denominators, distinct counts and medians are never summed into invented totals, and computational coverage does not establish source completeness. TL1 rates retain project/flavor/execution-class/version/configuration cohorts at attempt grain. Price comparison uses only historical and today-priced retained usage. Recently active work costs cover its lifetime. Repeated read counts do not establish unchanged-content retries. Distribution comparisons make no automatic statistical or causal claim.
